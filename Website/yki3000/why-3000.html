Learn useful words before rare ones.

The words we use most often do a lot of the everyday work: talking about people, explaining a problem, making plans, reading a message or sharing how we feel.

That’s why we focus on a practical collection of 3,000 words. Give your study a clear direction, build familiarity through repeated use, and let the words you know help you make sense of new ones in context.

A foundation for B1 (YKI level 3).

Build vocabulary for familiar situations and everyday communication as you work toward B1—the level corresponding to grade 3 in the YKI test. Take your learning into reading, listening, speaking and writing.

In either language edition, listen to the word and the complete sentence. Repeat it aloud and connect sound with meaning.

Give memory a picture.

Use illustrations, word-part links and mnemonic memory tricks. Then practise recalling the word before revealing it.

You know it when you hear it. Can you find it when you speak?

You might hear intohimo and recognise “passion”, yet draw a blank when you want to talk about your own passion in Finnish. Recognising a word from its meaning and producing the word from memory are related, but different, skills.

Our study cards start with English and ask you to recall the Finnish or Swedish word. That gives you practice in the direction you need when you want to express an idea: from a familiar meaning to a word in the language you are learning. For learners comfortable with English, it provides a clear starting cue.

Try it: look at passion, pause and try to say intohimo before revealing the answer. Make a connection through its parts: into = enthusiasm + himo = craving → intohimo = passion. Then listen and say the example aloud: Intohimo näkyy työssä. — “Passion shows in the work.” Practise bringing the Finnish word to mind from the meaning you want to express.

Research on vocabulary retrieval has found benefits from practising recall of the target-language form when learners are later asked to produce it. That supports our emphasis on productive recall for speaking and writing. It does not mean one direction is best for every learner or every skill: listening, reading and practice in both directions remain valuable.

Research: Yanagisawa (2016), The Effects of Receptive and Productive Word Retrieval Practice on Second Language Vocabulary Learning.
